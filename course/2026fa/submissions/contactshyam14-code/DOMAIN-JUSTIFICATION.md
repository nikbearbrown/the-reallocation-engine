# Domain justification — network targets for an Engineering Management graduate on 12-month OPT

## Executive summary

Who this recipe is for, what they can't see without it, and where it fits a job-search day. An engineering-management graduate whose one-year work permit starts at the end of December 2026 has a few months to find an employer who will sponsor a visa, and cannot easily tell which companies have actually sponsored project- or production-management work, which of those have nothing open now, or which sponsorship records belong to a different company. The recipe makes that visible from checked records, turns "sponsors, nothing open" into a networking list, and stops at the visa questions only a person can answer.

## Who, in exactly what situation

An international student finishing an MS in Engineering Management, starting 12-month post-completion F-1 OPT on 2026-12-31 (ending about 2027-12-30), no STEM extension claimed, targeting project management (SOC 13-1082) and industrial production management (SOC 11-3051). As they understand it (to confirm with the DSO), the 90 days of allowed unemployment count from the OPT start — a practical deadline near 2027-03-31 — and the year's H-1B registration is normally in March. So October 2026 to March 2027 is when the networking has to happen, and it must reach employers that sponsor *this* kind of work. Generic sponsor lists, dominated by software roles, describe someone else.

## The information asymmetry

1. **Who has sponsored a role like theirs.** Lists rank by volume; thousands of software approvals look like a safe bet for a project manager, and may not be.
2. **Who sponsors but has nothing open.** Invisible to a job-board search, yet the best informational-interview target: the conversation happens before the role is posted.
3. **Whose record is real.** In the shipped data, 42 groups (85 companies) share an identical sponsorship record under different names (CONVEY INC / COVEY INC), and all 1,557 approval and denial counts are even, which genuine counts would not be.

## Engine layers

**80 Days to Stay** (sponsorship history, funding columns — *record*); **Job-Ops** (board liveness via the repository's scanner, as a gate: a missing check holds a company, never counts as open); **The Cognitive Pivot** (BLS/O*NET SOC titles as match vocabulary only — role quality has zero scorer weight and is not used).

## Where it fits the 3-3-2 day

It takes over the **research half of the two research-and-apply hours** — which companies sponsor this kind of role, and whether each has a matching opening — and leaves tailoring to the person, after the visa-path gate. Its main output feeds the **three networking hours**: the "network first" list is who to ask for an informational conversation. Building and honestly testing it is credibility-hour work.

**Time saved — an estimate, not a measurement.** Assuming 12–18 minutes per company by hand (sponsorship history, funding, careers page) and about 20 companies a week: 4–6 hours by hand, versus about 1–1.5 hours with the recipe (seconds for the record steps; 3–5 minutes per board without a public API; the gate checks). About **3–4 hours a week**, not timed against a real week.

## Domain failure modes, and who would struggle to catch them

1. **A borrowed sponsorship record.** "38 approvals, 100% approved" on a clean row — but an upstream fuzzy join attached a lookalike company's record. Hardest for someone under time pressure; an AI summary repeats the row fluently. Flagged; identity is a human gate.
2. **A title that isn't the occupation.** "Technical Program Manager" reads like project management, but may have been a software role filed under a computer-occupation code. Hardest for non-software applicants, for whom that decides whether the record is relevant at all. Labelled an inference; ambiguous titles capped at a lower tier.
3. **"Sponsors" read as "safe", and timing ignored.** A 12-month OPT normally contains one H-1B registration window; an offer helps only if the employer registers the person in it and the registration is selected. A student who starts networking in April has missed it. The recipe never says a company will sponsor; it front-loads the networking list and holds tailoring for the DSO or attorney sign-off.
